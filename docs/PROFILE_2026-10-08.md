## What HeroClock is doing in this server

The capture includes `ArchRegistryWrapper.redirect$zpo000$heroclock$sizeFreshValues`, plus PropertyManager's `heroclock$reusePropertyLookup` and `heroclock$rememberPropertyLookup` handlers. This directly confirms execution of the registry allocation and property lookup hooks. KubeJS/Rhino boundary redirect methods and HeroClock's runtime tick handler also appear. The mod has not simply fallen back everywhere.

No `ArrayList.grow` frame is sampled beneath the suit provider, while live registry traversal still consumes 10.872 seconds / 6.12% there. That is consistent with the allocation patch's scope: it pre-sizes fresh snapshots and preserves traversal. Absence from samples does not prove zero allocation or a measured before/after gain. The remaining suit work includes 6.660 exclusive seconds in `HashBiMap.seekByKey`, 4.976 in `SuitSet.isWearing`, and 4.196 in `SuitSet.getChestplate` beneath the provider.

All frames whose class/method names contain `heroclock` total **0.048 exclusive seconds / 0.027%**. This is direct sampled attribution, not a complete overhead measurement: called library work and inlining can affect attribution. Do not blame HeroClock for the approximately 12.6% inclusive subtree behind its boundary wrappers; those wrappers invoke native script/gameplay work. The server work-queue tick handler itself has one 4 ms sample and is not a leading hotspot here. Wrapper presence does not establish whether optional telemetry collection was enabled.

The profile cannot report every compatibility decision, prove the seven-file OmniOptimizer resource selection, or certify behavior. `/heroclock status` and startup compatibility lines are the appropriate evidence for remaining gates; effective resources still determine which addon definitions execute.

## Next work

1. **Attribute and reduce recurring function/selector work.** The direct server function-tick subtree is 68.596 seconds / 38.58%, including 68.584 seconds in function execution. One particularly expensive branch goes through `SuperpowerCommand.lambda$register$8` to an entity selector and its NBT predicate: that predicate alone contributes 10.904 seconds, with 10.816 seconds in `Entity.m_20240_` entity serialization. Across all NBT selector branches, `PropertyManager.toNBT` contributes 7.344 seconds / 4.13%. Reducing the owning queries can reduce serialization without changing save semantics. Spark does not supply function IDs or selector strings for those samples; the previously audited Ninjago/FSang resources are candidates, not proven attribution of this exact branch.
2. **Review suit traversal and registry lookup as a separate major target.** The capacity hint is active but does not eliminate registry lookups, equipment suppliers or suit comparisons. Any further optimization needs correct handling of shared items, dynamic suppliers, subclasses, registry mutation and reload. The current one-to-one item-to-suit map is not sufficient justification for replacing the full scan.
3. **Continue validating the paused name comparison, with realistic expectations.** Of the name predicate's 13.152 seconds, 10.240 seconds / 5.76% lies in Component.getString beneath it. The prototype avoids constructing the flattened string while preserving traversal. Translation, callbacks, entity name construction and actual comparisons still cost time. Neither 7.40% nor 5.76% is a promised saving, and this patch alone is not a credible fix for roughly 10 TPS. Follow [NAME_SELECTOR_WIP.md](NAME_SELECTOR_WIP.md) before enabling or releasing it.

HeroClock 2.2.25 deliberately did not rewrite the large Ninjago/FSang workloads or the complete suit provider. Its resource-conflict fix and three redundant selector removals are narrow changes. This capture makes the remaining workload visible; it does not show that every previous fix failed.

The October 7 capture lacked HeroClock and used a different duration and workload. Do not convert differences in TPS or path percentages into a claimed improvement or regression. A matched comparison remains necessary to measure gains, but this capture is already sufficient to prioritize the next work. Custom KubeJS/Rhino replacement remains in Mantis.

No new runtime patch was made for this analysis. The name-selector prototype remains unvalidated on its WIP branch; 2.2.25 remains the tested build.
